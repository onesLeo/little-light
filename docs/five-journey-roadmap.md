# Little Light — five-journey roadmap

The first public store release is a complete five-journey volume. Each journey remains short,
gentle and fail-free: explore one place, find three meaningful objects, meet a Bible character,
complete one calm activity, hear a verse, and receive a virtue charm.

The first three journeys form a David story arc, including a reflective return to its beginning.
The last two open the Faith Journey into other Bible stories. The Wonder-Walker remains a guest in
each story rather than taking the Bible character's place.

| Journey | Place and passage | Virtue | Child's activity | Charm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. David's Valley | David prepares to face Goliath; 1 Samuel 17 and Joshua 1:9 | Courage | Find the stone, staff and lamb; complete Steady Hands | Courage |
| 2. The King's Camp | Jonathan befriends David; 1 Samuel 18:1–4 | Friendship | Find Jonathan's robe, bow and belt; tie three friendship loops | Friendship |
| 3. [The Beginning](chapter-3-concept.md) | Samuel anoints David; 1 Samuel 16:1–13 | Faithfulness (working choice) | Find signs of David's quiet work; help prepare the welcome for Samuel | Faithful Heart |
| 4. [Noah's Ark](chapter-4-concept.md) | Noah builds the ark and brings the animals aboard; Genesis 6–9 | Trust | Find building tools and bring matching animal pairs to the ark | Trust |
| 5. [Jonah and the Great Fish](chapter-5-concept.md) | Jonah runs, prays, receives another chance and goes to Nineveh; Jonah 1–4 | Mercy | Weather the storybook storm, pray in the deep and help Jonah take the road to Nineveh | Mercy |

## Journey 3 — The Beginning

Full visual, story, sound and implementation concept: [chapter-3-concept.md](chapter-3-concept.md).

After the King's Camp, Wonder Light opens an earlier part of the path: "Now let us go back to where
David's journey began." The setting is Bethlehem before the valley story. Samuel comes to Jesse's
home, sees David's older brothers, and learns that God does not judge the way people judge. David is
called in from caring for the sheep and Samuel anoints him.

**Recommended value: Faithfulness.** David is faithfully doing small, unseen work when Samuel comes.
The story's central truth is still the direct emphasis of the passage: people look at outward
appearance, but God sees the heart. "Faithful Heart" gives the charm a concrete child-facing name.

Other values considered:

- **Humility** fits David being the overlooked youngest brother, but the passage says more about how
  God sees than about David deliberately humbling himself.
- **Worth** supports the message that an overlooked child is seen, but it is less clearly a virtue
  the child can practise.
- **Chosen** should not be the charm value because it can sound as though God values one child above
  another.

Possible Wonder Items are David's small harp, a water bowl for the sheep, and his simple shepherd's
cloak. They show who David already is without repeating Chapter 1's stone, staff and lamb.

The signature activity can be **Prepare the Welcome**: place a cushion, water cup and oil lamp for
Samuel, then watch the expected choices pass by. The child does not choose the king by appearance
and does not perform the anointing. Samuel does that within the story.

Verse: 1 Samuel 16:7, using the project's chosen public-domain translation.

## Journey 4 — Noah's Ark

Full visual, story, sound and implementation concept: [chapter-4-concept.md](chapter-4-concept.md).

The journey opens on a broad dry plain with the ark already partly built. Noah trusts what God has
said and continues the long work. The chapter should include building, bringing animals aboard, a
gentle storybook transition through the rain, the dove's return and the rainbow. It should not show
people or animals in danger.

**Value: Trust.** Obedience is part of Noah's response, but trust explains why he keeps building even
before he can see what God has promised.

Possible Wonder Items are a wooden mallet, a coil of rope and a jar of pitch. The signature activity
is **Two by Two**: friendly animals arrive and the child matches pairs before they walk up the ramp.
There is no timer, wrong buzzer or animal left behind. The Trust charm can use the rainbow, while the
spoken explanation makes clear that the rainbow is God's promise rather than a prize Noah earned.

Candidate verse: Genesis 9:13. The narration can also quote or paraphrase Genesis 6:22 when Noah
finishes the work.

## Journey 5 — Jonah and the Great Fish

Full visual, story, sound and implementation concept: [chapter-5-concept.md](chapter-5-concept.md).
Experience and acceptance notes: [chapter-5-polish.md](chapter-5-polish.md).

The Bible calls the creature a **great fish**, so that is the chapter title. Store descriptions may
say "the story often called Jonah and the whale" so families can recognise it without changing the
wording inside the game.

The journey begins on a quiet quay at Joppa. Jonah looks toward a ship going the other way from
Nineveh. A stylised paper storm rises. The child secures Jonah's bag, message and lamp, stays on the
deck, and watches the sailors come through safely. The great fish keeps Jonah safe. Prayer is a
lifted paper page, not a place the child enters. Jonah is released onto the shore, and the child
carries the message with him to Nineveh. The plant, God's question and the city are the last picture,
so the chapter does not end at the fish.

**Value: Mercy.** God gives Jonah another chance, God keeps the sailors safe, and God cares about
the people Jonah does not want to forgive.

Wonder Items are Jonah's travel bag, a rolled message and a small oil lamp. They are also the cargo.
The signature activity is **Prayer in the Deep**: tap Call, Hear and Go toward Wonder Light. One
more word, Care, waits at the plant. The Mercy charm is a small warm light between two dark-blue
waves.

The played verse is the mercy clause of Jonah 4:2 (WEB). Jonah 4:11 and, if it passes a read-aloud
test, Jonah 2:2 live in the Faith Journal. Jonah 2:10 and Jonah's wish to die are not on the default
read-aloud path.

## Faith Journey map

The map expands from left to right:

1. The green valley and stream.
2. The blue-hour camp on the ridge.
3. A small Bethlehem home and sheep field, shown as an earlier page in David's story.
4. Noah's ark on a broad plain, with paired animals and a rainbow.
5. Joppa's harbour, the great fish in deep blue water, and the road to Nineveh.

Unfinished stops use the existing quiet preview treatment. A stop becomes playable only after its
journey is complete and its prerequisite journey has been finished by the current child.

## Shared production rules

- Target roughly 8–12 minutes per journey and about 45–60 minutes for the full first volume.
- Keep every activity fail-free, readable by touch, keyboard and gamepad, and understandable with
  read-aloud enabled.
- Reuse the checklist, camera director, dialogue, journal, charm ceremony, profiles and end card.
- Give each journey one distinct interaction so the structure feels familiar without becoming
  repetitive.
- Keep violence off-screen. Difficult choices can be named clearly without making harm playable.
- Keep accounts, advertising, child tracking and network access out of the first volume.
- Run a real-device family playtest after each journey. Do not postpone all validation until all five
  are built.

## Release gate

The paid store release waits until all five journeys are complete. Earlier builds may be shared as
private Android tests, TestFlight builds, or a clearly labelled free browser demo for research and
feedback. Payment integration begins only after family testing shows that children finish, replay
and ask to continue.

For the first paid release, prefer one upfront purchase containing all five journeys. A free first
journey plus a single Full Journey purchase remains a later option if testing shows that a free entry
point is necessary for discovery.
