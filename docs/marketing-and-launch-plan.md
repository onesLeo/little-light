# Little Light — marketing and launch plan

This plan turns the existing release direction into a practical path for testing interest, building
an audience and launching the first five-journey volume. It should be reviewed after every family
playtest rather than treated as a fixed campaign.

## Product promise

**A gentle, ad-free Bible adventure children can play and hear at their own pace.**

The clearest reasons for a parent, church or homeschool family to choose Little Light are:

- five short, child-friendly Bible journeys with no playable violence;
- spoken dialogue and read-aloud support;
- calm, fail-free activities rather than scores, pressure or punishment;
- local child profiles and progress, with no account, child tracking or network access;
- one complete purchase rather than ads, consumables or a subscription.

The primary buyer is a parent or caregiver. Children are the players. Secondary audiences are
Sunday-school leaders, churches, homeschool groups and Christian parenting creators. Marketing
should speak to the adult's trust questions first, then show the child the characters, exploration
and activities.

## Free and paid offer

The current project documents already make the right distinction:

| Stage | Offer | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Family research | Free, link-only browser demo; Chapters 1–2 may be available to invited families | Learn whether children understand, finish, replay and ask to continue |
| Public preview | Free browser demo with Journey 1, clearly labelled as a work in progress | Let interested families try the game without installing it |
| First store release | One upfront purchase containing all five journeys | A simple, parent-friendly premium product |
| Later option, only if needed | Free Journey 1 app with one parent-gated purchase to unlock the full volume | Improve discovery if the paid listing has too much friction |

Do not add advertising or a subscription to the first volume. A subscription only makes sense when
there is a reliable schedule of new content. If a free app plus unlock is tested later, the unlock is
digital content and must use the store's permitted in-app purchase flow. Keep purchasing and
external links behind the existing grown-ups area.

Do not choose the final price until family testing includes a willingness-to-pay question. A useful
first test is **US$4.99, US$6.99 and US$7.99**, localized by each store. Ask the parent which price
feels fair only after the child has played; do not ask the child. This is a research range, not a
committed launch price.

## The release funnel

### 1. Private family pilot

Recruit 10–20 families through people we already know, local churches, Sunday-school leaders and
homeschool communities. Give them the link-only browser build or a private Android/TestFlight
build. Observe at least five sessions rather than relying only on a survey.

Capture:

- demo opened → Journey 1 started → Journey 1 completed;
- whether the child starts Journey 2 or asks for another story;
- replay requests within seven days;
- places where an adult has to help;
- parent trust, clarity and willingness to recommend;
- price response after play;
- one optional parent quote, with written permission to publish it.

The product signal is not raw clicks. It is children finishing, replaying and asking to continue,
plus parents saying they would pay or recommend it.

### 2. Public-interest test

Create one simple parent-facing page with:

- a 15–30 second real-gameplay trailer;
- the five-journey map and the planned stories;
- the promise: ad-free, no playable violence, read aloud and progress kept on the device;
- age guidance based on the completed playtests;
- a **Play the free preview** button;
- a parent-only **Join the family beta / launch list** form;
- privacy, support and contact links.

Share it organically for four weeks before buying ads. Use a weekly rhythm: one gameplay clip, one
story or value post, one behind-the-scenes post and one parent/playtest learning. Good starting
channels are Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook parent/church groups, church newsletters,
homeschool communities and direct outreach to small Christian parenting creators.

Ask group administrators before posting. Give creators a private build and a short fact sheet; do
not script praise or pay for an undisclosed testimonial.

### 3. Store preparation and launch

After all five journeys pass the release gate:

1. Run an Android closed test and iOS TestFlight test on real family devices.
2. Prepare the privacy policy, support page, age declarations, data-safety answers, screenshots,
   trailer, icon, descriptions and signed builds.
3. Launch Android first if that remains the easiest supported build, then iPhone/iPad.
4. Use Google Play store-listing experiments and Apple product-page optimization to test one asset
   at a time: first screenshot, icon, then trailer.
5. Ask playtest families and partner communities to share the public store link at launch. Never
   ask for a positive rating; ask for an honest review.

Apple currently lists a US$99 annual Developer Program membership and a 15% commission for
approved Small Business Program members. Google Play has a 15% service-fee tier for the first
US$1 million after enrollment; fee programs and regional rules should be checked again immediately
before launch.

Because Little Light targets children, the listing and build must meet Apple Kids requirements and
Google Play Families requirements. The current local-only, no-ad design keeps this much simpler,
but a privacy policy and accurate store declarations are still required. Adult links and any future
purchase must use an effective parental gate.

## Paid promotion

Start paid promotion only after the organic page and demo show real interest. Use a small learning
budget, for example **US$150–300 total**, split across two hooks and two audience groups. Stop or
change the creative before increasing the budget if people click but do not start the demo or join
the list.

Test these audiences separately:

1. parents interested in Christian family resources and children's Bible activities;
2. homeschool and Sunday-school educators;
3. warm visitors who watched a video or visited the parent page, where local rules and platform
   policy allow it.

Aim advertising at adults. Do not target children, build child profiles for advertising, or install
ad-tracking SDKs in the game. Use page and store analytics at an aggregated campaign level.

Use this simple decision table:

| Signal | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Few people watch past 3 seconds | The hook is weak | Replace the opening, not the whole product |
| Video watches but few page visits | The call to action is unclear | Test a specific parent benefit and button |
| Page visits but few demo starts | Trust or load friction | Improve proof, device guidance and loading |
| Demo starts but few completions | Product issue | Fix the game before buying more traffic |
| Completions and replay, but low willingness to pay | Offer or price issue | Test price and free-first-journey packaging |

## Higgsfield promotional-video plan

The concrete game-side asset list, capture commands, character sheets and shot IDs are in
[promo-production-checklist.md](promo-production-checklist.md).

Higgsfield can support the campaign through Cinema Studio, Marketing Studio and motion/ad
variation workflows. Its own game-trailer guidance recommends locking character and location
references and writing a shot list before generation. Marketing Studio can then reuse the same
assets for short ad variants.

Use **real captured gameplay as the proof**. Use Higgsfield for the opening storybook moment, logo
motion, clean transitions, captions, aspect-ratio versions and optional cinematic teasers. Any
generated scene that is not in the game must be labelled as a storybook or concept teaser rather
than presented as gameplay.

Creative direction: classic, warm storybook motion; paper texture; gentle light; slow page turns;
steady camera moves; large readable words; no glitch, rapid kinetic text or aggressive trailer
editing.

### Master 15-second vertical preview

Format: 9:16, with safe framing that can also crop to 1:1. CTA during research: **Play the free
family preview**. CTA at paid launch: **Five Bible journeys. One gentle, ad-free game.**

| Time | Picture | Audio / words |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | A warm book opens into the Little Light valley | “What if a Bible story became a gentle adventure?” |
| 2–5 s | Real exploration and one Wonder Item found | Natural ambience and collection chime |
| 5–8 s | Real Steady Hands interaction | “Explore. Listen. Grow in courage.” |
| 8–11 s | David and Jonathan speaking with visible character motion | A short clean character line |
| 11–13 s | Faith Journey map with five stops | “Five journeys for young hearts.” |
| 13–15 s | Logo, trust line and CTA | “No ads. Read aloud. Play the preview.” |

### Campaign asset set

- one 30-second 16:9 gameplay trailer for the website and YouTube;
- one 15-second 9:16 master for Reels, Shorts and paid social;
- three 6–10 second cutdowns, each about one benefit: calm play, read aloud, or five Bible stories;
- one parent-trust clip: “No ads. No child tracking. Progress stays on the device.”;
- one clip for each completed journey, using its real environment and activity;
- store preview and screenshot motion concepts adapted to each store's current specifications.

Once one strong 4–30 second source clip exists, an ad-variation workflow can produce independent
hook, CTA and format versions while preserving the underlying gameplay. Generate only a few
meaningfully different variants, then let actual watch and conversion data decide what to make
next.
